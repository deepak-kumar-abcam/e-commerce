## How it fits together

- **AEM is the storefront, in two parts.** Intershop hosts no customer-facing pages. Marketing pages
  have moved to **AEM Edge Delivery Services**; checkout and the other commerce pages still run on
  **traditional AEM**, whose components call Intershop's REST APIs directly. Every page is planned
  to move to Edge Delivery Services. Customers sign in through the central Auth0 tenant.
- **Search runs in the page.** AEM pages load Coveo through the Coveo Headless and Atomic libraries.
  Coveo indexes product data, fed directly by inRiver, and AEM content.
- **Commerce consumes data it doesn't own.** Products are created in each OpCo's ERP and enriched in
  inRiver, the central PIM, before they reach Intershop and Coveo. Customer-specific prices and
  quotes come from the ERP; customers and segments from the CRM. See the [flow diagrams](/flows/).
- **Inbound data travels as files.** ERPs drop files on the Danaher Life Sciences SFTP server. For
  Phenomenex, pricing, quotes, customers, and segments are staged in WebDB first, which sends them
  on to the SFTP server — pricing and quotes on scheduled jobs, one per data type. Boomi picks the files up and delivers them to inRiver and
  Intershop.
- **Everything behind the sale differs per OpCo.** Orders go to each OpCo's own ERP, payment runs
  through its own provider, and campaigns through its own marketing platform. Danaher Life Sciences
  has **no ERP** yet, because no legal entity has been established, and **takes no payments**,
  because it doesn't transact directly.
- **CRM is converging on Salesforce.** Danaher Life Sciences, SCIEX, and Leica Microsystems use
  Salesforce; Phenomenex uses Microsoft Dynamics CRM and plans to move to Salesforce.
