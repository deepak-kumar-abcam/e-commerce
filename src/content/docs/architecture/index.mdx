The central Intershop instance is one part of a wider set of systems, most of them shared by every
operating company on it and run by the platform team. This page maps them; the
[flow diagrams](/flows/) follow each kind of data through them hop by hop.

## How it fits together

- **AEM is the storefront, in two parts.** Intershop hosts no customer-facing pages. Every page
  except checkout runs on **AEM Edge Delivery Services**; checkout still runs on **traditional
  AEM**, and is planned to move too. Both call Intershop's REST APIs and Auth0 directly.
- **Product content comes from Coveo; transactional data from Intershop.** AEM pages load Coveo
  through the Coveo Headless and Atomic libraries, and it is their only source of product content —
  inRiver doesn't feed AEM. Intershop supplies the transactional data: list price, unit of measure,
  minimum order quantity, and customer-specific price. Coveo indexes product data, fed directly by
  inRiver, and AEM content, which AEM pushes to it on a scheduled sync.
- **Customers sign in through one Auth0 tenant.** Every OpCo uses the same Universal Login pages,
  with its own branding; custom forms collect OpCo-specific answers, which reach the storefront in
  the ID token. Intershop's custom token handler creates the user and customer from it. See the
  [sign-in flow](/flows/sign-in/).
- **Commerce consumes data it doesn't own.** Products are created in each OpCo's ERP and enriched in
  inRiver, the central PIM, before they reach Intershop and Coveo. Danaher Life Sciences has no ERP:
  it is a common marketplace hosting the other OpCos' products, fed from the same inRiver.
  Customer-specific prices and quotes come from the ERP; customers and segments from the CRM. See
  the [flow diagrams](/flows/).
- **Inbound data travels as files.** ERPs drop files on the Danaher Life Sciences SFTP server — for
  SCIEX, customer-specific prices on a scheduled Oracle job, over SSH. For Phenomenex, pricing,
  quotes, customers, and segments are staged in WebDB first, which sends them on to the SFTP
  server — pricing and quotes on scheduled jobs, one per data type. Boomi picks the files up and
  delivers them to inRiver and Intershop.
- **Orders go out by direct API.** Intershop calls each OpCo's ERP itself, with no Boomi or files —
  except Leica Microsystems, whose orders are emailed to customer service until a REST API to SAP
  arrives, planned for later in 2026.
  For Phenomenex it calls the ERP twice: **Order Simulate** during checkout, for tax, shipping, an
  estimated delivery date, and any account block; then to create the order in the ERP's pre-order
  table, from which a batch job makes a sales order. SCIEX orders go to Oracle's order-create API,
  where they are booked directly or flagged for customer service to review. See the
  [orders flow](/flows/orders/).
- **Everything behind the sale differs per OpCo.** Orders go to each OpCo's own ERP, payment runs
  through its own provider, and campaigns through its own marketing platform. Danaher Life Sciences
  has **no ERP** yet, because no legal entity has been established, and **takes no payments**,
  because it doesn't transact directly. See the [payments flow](/flows/payments/).
- **CRM is converging on Salesforce, one org per OpCo.** Danaher Life Sciences, SCIEX, and Leica
  Microsystems each have their own Salesforce org; Phenomenex uses Microsoft Dynamics CRM, and its
  move to Salesforce is in planning, expected around the third quarter of 2027.
