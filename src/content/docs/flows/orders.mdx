Orders are the first **outbound** flow: they start in Intershop and end in the operating company's
ERP. Unlike the inbound feeds, they travel by direct API call — no Boomi, no files — except at
Leica Microsystems, where they still go by email.

For **Phenomenex**, the ERP is involved twice. During checkout, Intershop calls the ERP's **Order
Simulate** API, which returns tax, shipping, and an estimated delivery date — and stops the order if
the customer's account has a block in the ERP. When the customer places the order, Intershop creates
it directly in the ERP's pre-order table, and a batch job converts it into a sales order. Orders
with missing information, such as a customer who is new and not yet in the ERP, are flagged for the
order booking team to resolve.

For **SCIEX**, Intershop creates the order through Oracle's REST order-create API, with no check
against Oracle during checkout. Oracle books it directly when the account, ship-to, and bill-to
already exist and it is a normal order within a threshold value. Flags set up by customer service
hold everything else for manual review: a new account or address, a special request such as
shipping instructions or notes, or a value over the threshold.

For **Leica Microsystems**, Intershop emails each order to the customer service team today. Sending
it to SAP through a REST API is planned for later in 2026.

Danaher Life Sciences takes no direct orders. The diagram below follows Phenomenex's route; the
hop table covers every route. Card payments, authorised when the
order is placed and captured on invoice, are covered by the [payments flow](/flows/payments/).
