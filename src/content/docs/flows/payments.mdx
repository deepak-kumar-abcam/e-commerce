Card payments follow the same shape for every OpCo that takes them. At checkout the card is
entered in an iframe served by the payment provider, and saved so the customer can use it again.
When the order is placed, Intershop has the provider authorise an amount on the card, and if the
provider declines, the order can't be placed. The real amount is captured once the order is
invoiced. The OpCos differ in which provider they use, how much they authorise, and who captures.

- **Phenomenex** uses **Stripe**. A SetupIntent saves the card to Stripe, and the order total is
  authorised for manual capture. The PaymentIntent and payment method IDs go to the ERP with the
  order. When the order is invoiced, the ERP captures through Stripe. It can capture less or more
  than was authorised, or capture several times for a split shipment; over-capture and
  multicapture are enabled on the account by special request to Stripe, and so is extended
  authorisation. If an authorisation still expires, the ERP creates a new PaymentIntent on the
  payment method it received with the order. It then records a payment journal against the paid
  invoice. Refunds go through the same integration.
- **Leica Microsystems** uses **Stripe** the same way up to the authorisation. Capture is
  **manual**: customer service invoices the order and emails finance, who capture the amount in the
  Stripe Dashboard, or charge the saved card afresh if the authorisation has expired. Refunds are
  made by hand in the Dashboard too. An SAP integration is planned but not yet on the roadmap.
- **SCIEX** uses **Cybersource**, for cards in the US and Canada, through Intershop's Cybersource
  connector. Intershop exchanges Microform's short-lived token for a stored token and saves it as
  a payment instrument. When the order is placed, only **$0.10** is authorised. The order goes to
  the ERP with the card details and stored token, and once it is invoiced the ERP authorises and
  captures the invoiced amount afresh. Refunds are made by hand in Cybersource.

The Stripe integration is the platform team's own (see [custom features](/custom-features/)):
Intershop's Stripe connector doesn't support SetupIntents, so it can't save cards in Stripe for
later charges. Each OpCo has its own Stripe organisation, with several
accounts to sell globally, and the Stripe settings live in Intershop managed services, one per
sales channel. 3-D Secure applies to Stripe customers in the EU and Australia. No Stripe webhooks
are used.

Every OpCo that takes orders also offers **pay by invoice**: the customer gives a purchase order
number and can upload the purchase order document, which goes to the ERP as binary data in the
order-create API call. No payment provider is involved, so it isn't part of this flow.

Danaher Life Sciences takes no direct payments. How orders reach the ERP is covered by the
[orders flow](/flows/orders/).
